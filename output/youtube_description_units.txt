CPU, GPU, TPU, NPU, LPU, wafer-scale engine, FPGA, ASIC and DPU: what each one actually is, explained with real specs instead of buzzwords. One yardstick runs through the whole video: how fast each chip could generate text with an 8-billion-parameter AI model, which comes down to memory bandwidth ÷ model size.

CHIPS USED AS EXAMPLES
• CPU: AMD EPYC 9654 (96 cores, 12-channel DDR5, 384 MB L3, 360 W)
• GPU: NVIDIA H100 SXM (16,896 CUDA cores, 528 Tensor Cores, 80 GB HBM3, 700 W)
• TPU: Google TPU v5e (197 TFLOP/s BF16, 16 GB HBM, pods of 256 chips)
• NPU: Qualcomm Snapdragon X Elite (45 TOPS INT8, ~135 GB/s LPDDR5X)
• LPU: Groq (230 MB on-chip SRAM, 80 TB/s)
• Wafer-scale: Cerebras WSE-3 (46,225 mm², 4 trillion transistors, 900,000 cores)
• ASIC: Bitmain Antminer S21 (200 TH/s, 3,500 W)
• DPU: NVIDIA BlueField-3 (400 Gb/s, 16 Arm cores)

Speeds are theoretical ceilings for an 8B model stored at 8 bits (8 GB read per token); real systems land below them. Figures are vendor-published specs.
